# PitCrew research: how drivers actually get faster

PitCrew exists to make its users better drivers. This document distils what two coaching channels teach about building driving skill, and turns it into things PitCrew can measure, detect and coach.

| Channel | Who | Lens |
|---|---|---|
| [SimRacing Arnout](https://www.youtube.com/@SimracingArnout) | Arnout Hoekstra, setup builder and self-described "2 seconds off the aliens" club racer | Setup as a training tool, consistency, mindset, racecraft for time-poor amateurs |
| [Suellio Almeida](https://www.youtube.com/@SuellioAlmeida) | Coach with 4,000+ hours of one-to-one sessions; went from sim racer to Radical North America champion and IMSA driver | Tyre physics, input technique, motor learning, a structured skill ladder |

> **Method.** Both channels were indexed in full (217 + 204 videos). Hardware and VR reviews, news, race vlogs and track-by-track setup uploads were left out, which leaves 235 videos about driving skill. Of those, 219 have English transcripts. I read 58 of the most technique-dense ones in full, with timestamps, and searched the rest by theme. 19 relevant videos are missing because YouTube blocked further downloads with a bot check (listed under [Gaps](#gaps)). Every claim links to the second of video it comes from. Quotes are lightly cleaned auto-captions.

## 1. The ten findings that matter most

1. **Lap time comes from the middle of the corner, not from braking late.** Both creators say late braking should be the *last* skill you work on. You first learn how much the car can rotate and at what minimum speed, then move the brake point back until it starts to interfere ([Suellio 1:02](https://youtu.be/PDsNPRJxjd8?t=62), [Suellio 1:00](https://youtu.be/Tr3K0ow-1-0?t=60), [Suellio 7:16](https://youtu.be/cMsM_uMxH34?t=436)). Minimum speed is where beginners and pros differ most "in about 99% of corners" ([Suellio 17:20](https://youtu.be/Tr3K0ow-1-0?t=1040)).
2. **Consistency comes before speed, and consistency is built at the brake pedal.** "90% of your consistency comes from having the car always slow down the same way" ([Suellio 0:31](https://youtu.be/uceoiawBBcM?t=31)). Arnout's own ladder is consistency → awareness → adaptability ([Arnout 4:35](https://youtu.be/ZxcTrq03TwY?t=275)).
3. **Entry is the cause and exit is the consequence.** Most exit spins start in the late-entry phase ([Suellio 3:05](https://youtu.be/FjyjbD_dJAU?t=185)). Suellio has worked with students on entries alone for months, and their exits fixed themselves ([Suellio 8:39](https://youtu.be/wrKAwiOnL4U?t=519)).
4. **Brakes and steering work as one control.** Brake pressure multiplies what the steering does ("~10× more rotation from the same angle" under trail braking, [Suellio 1:02](https://youtu.be/WrbV_vmuq_A?t=62)). Adding steering while understeering does nothing ([Suellio 0:30](https://youtu.be/MMvxErwpxxw?t=30)). Adding heavy brake while keeping the same steering lock locks the tyres or spins the car ([Suellio 7:44](https://youtu.be/MMvxErwpxxw?t=464)).
5. **The lower half of brake pressure matters more than the upper half.** 0–20% mid-corner and 20–50% at turn-in is where rotation comes from. Drivers who brake "100 then 0" lose seconds ([Suellio 5:04](https://youtu.be/Tr3K0ow-1-0?t=304), [Suellio 2:06](https://youtu.be/0cX116actow?t=126)). Each creator coaches the same drill: hold 1% on the pedal ([Suellio 8:19](https://youtu.be/uceoiawBBcM?t=499), [Arnout 3:42](https://youtu.be/KlVMVVnQd20?t=222)).
6. **Relax your hands to read the car.** Suellio's light-firm-light pattern: relax whenever weight shifts to the front tyres, grip firmer at minimum speed, relax again on exit. It is his single most-repeated tip, appearing in 40 of his videos ([Suellio 0:31](https://youtu.be/WrbV_vmuq_A?t=31), [Suellio 9:15](https://youtu.be/MMvxErwpxxw?t=555)).
7. **Training to the limit beats driving under it.** "If you can cause it, you can prevent it": spin on purpose, induce understeer on purpose ([Suellio 9:46](https://youtu.be/hHiDdrgpTGQ?t=586), [Suellio 9:09](https://youtu.be/wrKAwiOnL4U?t=549)). A driver who stays under the limit can be inconsistent without being punished, then makes one big mistake ([Suellio 9:19](https://youtu.be/zFALqnm5MDw?t=559)).
8. **Experiment with one narrow thing at a time instead of grinding laps.** Your conscious attention handles only about 50 bits a second ([Arnout 5:12](https://youtu.be/cqZ3w2Bf-ik?t=312)). Blind laps lock bad habits in ([Suellio 2:36](https://youtu.be/SxUSZVKpHAE?t=156), [Arnout 3:33](https://youtu.be/dG95VpmDSno?t=213)).
9. **Racing teaches more than solo practice, as long as the variables stay fixed.** Run the same car on the same track across several races and your lap time drops (1:41.4 → 1:40.9, P3 in split 1, [Arnout 2:30](https://youtu.be/Xic-gKE0788?t=150)). Running a full season in one car took 0.9 s off Brands Hatch months later ([Arnout 6:41](https://youtu.be/Qi8F5PAGda8?t=401)).
10. **Copying is not understanding.** Track guides and telemetry overlays show you *what* a fast driver did, not *why*. Both creators warn that copying brake points without the principle behind them leads to a plateau ([Suellio 5:38](https://youtu.be/SxUSZVKpHAE?t=338), [Suellio 1:02](https://youtu.be/LZanXwUDnLI?t=62), [Arnout 0:30](https://youtu.be/P63-bcpWsKc?t=30)).

## 3. Skill ladders with measurable targets

Both creators describe progression as a ladder in which each rung unlocks the next. Trail braking taught too early slows people down. "If you're 3 seconds off, trail braking is not going to fix it" ([Suellio 3:37](https://youtu.be/SxUSZVKpHAE?t=217)).

| Rung | Arnout: gap to the pace ([Arnout 2:36](https://youtu.be/0tpqhKxF1yc?t=156)) | Suellio: course level and benchmark ([Suellio 7:10](https://youtu.be/nUpAKh2tZB8?t=430)) | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **"Wildly off."** Late braking, steering corrections, overshot apexes, on-off throttle | **Safety & Consistency.** Top 10% globally, 3–4 s off, 10–20 consistent laps | Brake early with one input at a time (brake, turn, commit to throttle). Fixed brake references, a chosen gear per corner, turn assists off, stay on track |
| 2 | **~5 s off.** Throttle comes too late: corner not opened up, or too much mid-corner speed | **Balance & Speed.** Top 5%, ~2 s off | Copy *lines* (not brake points) from guides, then learn the brake release and trail braking ("about half a year") |
| 3 | **3–4 s off.** "Most sim racers are stuck here" ([Arnout 13:54](https://youtu.be/0tpqhKxF1yc?t=834)) | **Cornering Precision.** Top 2% | Spirals, maximum rotation point, compound and blind corners, checkpoints; step up to a more aggressive setup |
| 4 | **Split 1.** "0.5 s at level 4 equals the whole gain from L1 to L3" ([Arnout 19:34](https://youtu.be/0tpqhKxF1yc?t=1174)) | **Mastery.** Top 1% | Neutral steer, dynamic brake bias, active driving, cold tyres, tyre temperatures |

Suellio's three-step limit model ([Suellio 1:01](https://youtu.be/6MqJRQbAY0U?t=61)) explains where people stall:

1. **Overslow, coast, throttle early.** A safe platform but a low minimum speed.
2. **Carry speed in, throttle from a higher minimum.** This needs braking while turning, the steering becomes very sensitive ("5° with brakes > 40° without"), and the line changes shape. This is where most drivers get stuck: at ABS, extra steering does nothing, while 10% less brake can spin the car, and most beginners can't yet feel 10% on the pedal ([Suellio 9:13](https://youtu.be/6MqJRQbAY0U?t=553)).
3. **Maximise lateral load at all times.** Keep the front tyres working and correct quickly ([Suellio 15:49](https://youtu.be/6MqJRQbAY0U?t=949)).

## 4. The technique model PitCrew should coach

### 4.1 Corner phases ([Suellio 0:00](https://youtu.be/FjyjbD_dJAU?t=0))

The corner runs **hard braking → early entry → late entry → (maximum rotation point) → early exit → late exit**.

- **Early entry.** Release the brake as you add steering, "like pouring liquid from one cup to another". Never hold a fixed pressure while turning, and never reapply the brake once the car is rotating. The most common error here is steering too much or too fast.
- **Late entry.** Build rotation up to the maximum rotation point. The common error is passive driving: understeering and waiting for the car to turn.
- **Early exit.** Throttle on and rotation decreasing, but don't unwind the steering too fast.
- **Late exit.** This phase reaps the earlier ones. **Understeer on early exit followed by snap oversteer on late exit** accounts for "almost 100%" of exit spins ([Suellio 5:05](https://youtu.be/FjyjbD_dJAU?t=305)).
- **Where it is safe to push:** late entry and early exit. **Where to be careful:** early entry and late exit ([Suellio 7:07](https://youtu.be/FjyjbD_dJAU?t=427), [Suellio 17:55](https://youtu.be/PDsNPRJxjd8?t=1075)).

| Application | Fast, not "hard": reach peak pressure *at* the reference, within ≤0.5 s | [Suellio 4:14](https://youtu.be/uceoiawBBcM?t=254), [Suellio 3:08](https://youtu.be/51Dw_vhcUBA?t=188), [Suellio 6:58](https://youtu.be/K7IVcD-TF3Y?t=418) |

| Hold | Maintain it. A dip and reapply arrives 5–10 km/h too fast | [Suellio 5:14](https://youtu.be/uceoiawBBcM?t=314), [Suellio 17:04](https://youtu.be/uceoiawBBcM?t=1024) |

### 4.3 Rotation: three tools, two pedals, one wheel

- **The three tools are steering, trail braking and engine braking** ([Suellio 21:58](https://youtu.be/45I_XjxnAgw?t=1318)). In Suellio's telemetry the answer is almost always in those three traces. One student steered twice as much as the reference and stayed in 2nd gear instead of 1st, which cost 0.8 s in one corner ([Suellio 23:29](https://youtu.be/45I_XjxnAgw?t=1409)).
- **Balance the car with the pedals, not the wheel.** Through the middle 50% of the corner, 10% brake fixes understeer and 10% throttle fixes oversteer ("negative brakes") ([Suellio 6:10](https://youtu.be/_40IrLOww9s?t=370), [Suellio 33:26](https://youtu.be/f7VwINirLuM?t=2006)). Countersteer is the last resort and costs ~0.5 s ([Suellio 11:53](https://youtu.be/_40IrLOww9s?t=713)).
- **Never add steering when understeering.** The front tyres are saturated, so load them instead by lifting or adding a little brake ([Suellio 1:02](https://youtu.be/MMvxErwpxxw?t=62)). You can recognise understeer when more steering doesn't change the yaw rate ([Suellio 4:11](https://youtu.be/MMvxErwpxxw?t=251)).
- **Be afraid of the right things.** Many drivers steer too little mid-corner. "Overdrive the steering, not the brakes or throttle" ([Suellio 10:50](https://youtu.be/_40IrLOww9s?t=650)). Each 5° of rotation you miss at low speed has to be found at higher speed later, which is how late-exit spins happen ([Suellio 5:38](https://youtu.be/_40IrLOww9s?t=338)).

### 4.4 The line

- **Spirals.** Under braking the line is a closing spiral (steering keeps increasing). Under acceleration it is an opening spiral (steering keeps decreasing). A constant arc only exists at constant speed ([Suellio 16:30](https://youtu.be/f7VwINirLuM?t=990)). Many beginners hold a fixed steering angle on power, which produces understeer ([Suellio 19:03](https://youtu.be/f7VwINirLuM?t=1143)).
- **The line follows from speed.** At the right speed the car carries itself to the outside on exit. Unwinding the wheel to "use all the track" while under the limit is a trap ([Suellio 7:31](https://youtu.be/YozWiTOLi6U?t=451), [Suellio 25:48](https://youtu.be/f7VwINirLuM?t=1548)). To test it, steer a little more after getting on power. If the car accepts it, carry more speed next lap ([Suellio 6:28](https://youtu.be/YozWiTOLi6U?t=388)).
- **Track usage still pays.** Leaving half a car width on entry is worth ~0.1 s per corner ([Suellio 9:45](https://youtu.be/PDsNPRJxjd8?t=585)), or 0.7 s on a 40 s lap ([Suellio 9:05](https://youtu.be/45I_XjxnAgw?t=545)). Video shows this better than telemetry ([Suellio 11:08](https://youtu.be/45I_XjxnAgw?t=668)).
- **Compound corners.** Reverse-engineer back from the corner before the longest straight and set checkpoints. Never drive straight between two corners ([Suellio 4:06](https://youtu.be/KPo8GeGKGhk?t=246), [Suellio 28:19](https://youtu.be/f7VwINirLuM?t=1699)).
- **Downforce shapes the line.** High downforce gives a U shape with linear steering and one apex. Low downforce gives a V shape with progressive steering, an earlier turn-in and two apexes ([Suellio 4:05](https://youtu.be/6MqJRQbAY0U?t=245)).
- **Vision.** Look half a second ahead: once you reach a target, move your eyes to the next one. One student gained 4.5 s from vision coaching alone ([Suellio 4:58](https://youtu.be/gIl5b8_0h_8?t=298), [Suellio 24:51](https://youtu.be/gIl5b8_0h_8?t=1491)).

### 4.5 Light hands ([Suellio 0:31](https://youtu.be/WrbV_vmuq_A?t=31))

- **Light on entry, firm at minimum speed, light on exit.** Relax whenever weight moves forward.
- **Passive countersteer.** Relaxed hands let the force feedback catch slides ([Suellio 6:09](https://youtu.be/WrbV_vmuq_A?t=369)).
- **"Hyper-obsessing over the apex"** makes drivers grip firmly and spin. If you arrive too fast, give up the apex ([Suellio 4:07](https://youtu.be/WrbV_vmuq_A?t=247)).
- **Force feedback is the main source of information.** Peak resistance comes slightly before peak grip ([Suellio 5:38](https://youtu.be/DT-1REn31eQ?t=338)). The wheel going light means understeer ([Suellio 2:35](https://youtu.be/3MLKewyTanc?t=155)). "Fishing for grip" means searching for the steering angle with the most force ([Arnout 21:22](https://youtu.be/DMGBVH7IGBY?t=1282)).

> **What the scoring should reward.** Suellio's "John vs Marco" story is the warning ([Suellio 5:38](https://youtu.be/SxUSZVKpHAE?t=338)). John practised until his brake trace looked beautiful, then plateaued 0.2 s behind Marco, who chased tyre force and let the trace follow. **PitCrew should score outcomes (minimum speed, exit speed, consistency) and use trace shape only to explain them.** It should never reward beautiful traces for their own sake.

## 6. Drill library

Each drill comes straight from the videos. PitCrew can turn each one into a guided session with a measurable pass condition.

| Drill | How | Pass condition | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1% pedal** | In the pits or on a straight, hold barely 1% on the brake meter, then step 1% up or down | Hold 1/25/50/75/99% each for 2 s, then release 1% at a time | [Suellio 8:19](https://youtu.be/uceoiawBBcM?t=499), [Suellio 4:00](https://youtu.be/RK6kE0ftFA0?t=240) |
| **Broken brakes** | Never brake above 30% all lap. Brake earlier and feel the rotation | A full lap with no off and peak brake ≤ 30% | [Suellio 6:16](https://youtu.be/0cX116actow?t=376) |
| **Never 100%** | Cap brake at 50–60%. Suellio beat a student by >1 s doing this | A lap with no lockup and minimum speed ≥ baseline | [Suellio 3:34](https://youtu.be/Tr3K0ow-1-0?t=214) |
| **Brake earlier, release earlier** | Move the brake point *earlier* than feels right and release earlier | Exit speed ↑, lap time ≤ baseline | [Arnout 28:03](https://youtu.be/_PP-4ogMwHA?t=1683) |
| **Spin on purpose** | Spin on purpose on corner entry (a little brake plus a little steering, not lots of either) | Three intentional spins on entry, zero unintentional | [Suellio 0:31](https://youtu.be/tmnlQOfjDKI?t=31), [Suellio 6:12](https://youtu.be/LZanXwUDnLI?t=372) |

## 7. How to practise

Both channels spend as much time on *how to practise* as on *what to do*. These are the guidelines PitCrew should build its sessions around.

- **Change behaviour, get feedback, learn.** "If you keep doing the same thing, nothing changes" ([Arnout 3:07](https://youtu.be/0tpqhKxF1yc?t=187)). Make a **narrow prediction**: one testable change, knowing many will be wrong ([Arnout 4:39](https://youtu.be/cqZ3w2Bf-ik?t=279), [Arnout 6:13](https://youtu.be/cqZ3w2Bf-ik?t=373)).
- **Awareness first.** After a lap, reflect on your *inputs*, not the lap time ([Arnout 4:05](https://youtu.be/dG95VpmDSno?t=245)). Fast drivers can perceive several states per corner, such as understeer on entry and oversteer on exit. Treat that "refresh rate" as a skill to grow ([Suellio 12:23](https://youtu.be/zFALqnm5MDw?t=743)).
- **Fix the variables.** One sim, one car, one track, default force feedback, until it gets boring ([Arnout 4:37](https://youtu.be/Hi8kqPq8VpU?t=277), [Arnout 1:30](https://youtu.be/ZxcTrq03TwY?t=90)). Your first goal is three laps without going off ([Arnout 3:33](https://youtu.be/ZxcTrq03TwY?t=213)). You are ready to race after five clean consecutive laps ([Arnout 1:32](https://youtu.be/IetFspa2BJ8?t=92)).
- **Learn a track in short blocks.** Drive the first laps slowly with no guide and the track map enlarged. Then do 30–45 minute blocks, watch the guide off the rig, and return ([Arnout 1:00](https://youtu.be/D_CT0qrxQz0?t=60), [Arnout 9:18](https://youtu.be/D_CT0qrxQz0?t=558)). The second track is learned about three times faster ([Arnout 5:40](https://youtu.be/ZxcTrq03TwY?t=340)). Suellio aims for 2–5 laps, or about 30 minutes, once the method is internalised ([Suellio 12:45](https://youtu.be/nUpAKh2tZB8?t=765)).
- **Your best lap comes early.** Peak pace usually arrives on laps 2–4 of a fresh session and then fades ([Arnout 9:48](https://youtu.be/Qi8F5PAGda8?t=588)). Long solo sessions rarely add pace; races with something at stake do ([Arnout 0:30](https://youtu.be/Xic-gKE0788?t=30)). Race online early, because it teaches 2–4× faster than practice ([Arnout 6:11](https://youtu.be/Hi8kqPq8VpU?t=371)).
- **Spaced repetition.** Being re-exposed to the same idea flattens the forgetting curve ([Suellio 13:18](https://youtu.be/hHiDdrgpTGQ?t=798)). Muscle memory needs four things: time, patience, a reference and repetition ([Suellio 10:16](https://youtu.be/hHiDdrgpTGQ?t=616)). It fades after breaks "like elastic" ([Suellio 14:24](https://youtu.be/MMvxErwpxxw?t=864)).
- **Watch replay before telemetry.** Watch your own replay first, then compare with telemetry, and always ask *why* ([Suellio 2:00](https://youtu.be/45I_XjxnAgw?t=120)). Suellio discourages overlaying telemetry while learning because "it tells you what's wrong, not why" ([Suellio 5:42](https://youtu.be/cMsM_uMxH34?t=342)). Arnout uses an input overlay to reflect after each corner ([Arnout 14:35](https://youtu.be/DMGBVH7IGBY?t=875)). PitCrew should go between the two: explain the cause and the principle, not just the difference from a reference.
- **Get out of the way of the plateau.** Plateaus are most common around 2 s off the pace. They come from repeating the wrong things or from jumping ahead of the fundamentals ([Suellio 1:02](https://youtu.be/SxUSZVKpHAE?t=62)). They break with aha moments, not effort ([Suellio 8:15](https://youtu.be/hHiDdrgpTGQ?t=495)). "Stop trying too hard" ([Suellio 1:32](https://youtu.be/hHiDdrgpTGQ?t=92)).

## 8. Mindset and racecraft

- **Frame the task, not yourself.** Say "that lap was bad", not "I am bad". Say "my trail braking is *currently* bad", not "I'm a bad trail braker" ([Suellio 0:31](https://youtu.be/hHiDdrgpTGQ?t=31), [Suellio 11:18](https://youtu.be/hHiDdrgpTGQ?t=678)).
- **Confidence means knowing what happens next.** It comes from repetition and a track record of finished races ([Arnout 2:02](https://youtu.be/W0Cra-8zqig?t=122)). Fear comes from unpredictability, and approaching the limit methodically removes it ([Suellio 3:00](https://youtu.be/wrKAwiOnL4U?t=180)).
- **Lap time is information, not a verdict.** Being 5 s off and 1.2 s off feel the same ([Arnout 9:15](https://youtu.be/cqZ3w2Bf-ik?t=555)).
- **Take ownership of incidents.** Assuming the incident was your fault gives you control. Review the replay, and read your opponents' behaviour ([Arnout 1:00](https://youtu.be/jVz-u8R2Vi4?t=60), [Arnout 8:35](https://youtu.be/jVz-u8R2Vi4?t=515)).
- **For rookies, finishing is the goal.** Aim to finish races, be passive and predictable at starts, and let impatient drivers pass ([Arnout 2:02](https://youtu.be/W0Cra-8zqig?t=122), [Arnout 8:12](https://youtu.be/IetFspa2BJ8?t=492)).
- **Specific racecraft:**
  - lift half a second before your reference when following ([Suellio 1:31](https://youtu.be/R6yb7Fm8m6o?t=91))
  - release the brake to avoid a car closing fast behind ([Suellio 0:31](https://youtu.be/R6yb7Fm8m6o?t=31))
  - don't stare at cars ([Suellio 2:33](https://youtu.be/R6yb7Fm8m6o?t=153))
  - leave one car width ([Suellio 4:36](https://youtu.be/R6yb7Fm8m6o?t=276))
  - set up passes two or three corners ahead ([Suellio 14:20](https://youtu.be/KPo8GeGKGhk?t=860))
  - pressure without attacking until the driver ahead cracks ([Arnout 2:35](https://youtu.be/r-ZfkwV4PC4?t=155))

## 9. Pre-flight checks (hardware and settings)

Several coaching sessions found that the "driving problem" was really a settings problem. PitCrew should check these before diagnosing technique:

- **Brake pedal:**
  - holds 1–3% without jumping to 10–20%
  - no spikes and no collapse on release ([Arnout 1:03](https://youtu.be/KlVMVVnQd20?t=63))
  - 100% sits at the end of travel ([Arnout 6:19](https://youtu.be/KlVMVVnQd20?t=379))
  - load cell set linear ([Suellio 4:30](https://youtu.be/RK6kE0ftFA0?t=270))
  - not saturating at 100% ([Suellio 18:42](https://youtu.be/0cX116actow?t=1122))
- **Throttle reaches 100%.** Arnout spent weeks hunting top speed in his setup before finding his pedal stopped at 99% ([Arnout 6:50](https://youtu.be/P63-bcpWsKc?t=410)).
- **Steering lock** matches the real car ([Arnout 4:04](https://youtu.be/h_Yus3kgJHs?t=244)).
- **Force feedback** doesn't clip for more than a moment ([Suellio 6:11](https://youtu.be/3MLKewyTanc?t=371)), and the same base settings are kept across sims ([Arnout 6:37](https://youtu.be/h_Yus3kgJHs?t=397)). Linear mode stays on and effects stay minimal ([Suellio 11:48](https://youtu.be/3MLKewyTanc?t=708)).
- **Field of view** comes from a calculator, at most +10% ([Suellio 3:38](https://youtu.be/51Dw_vhcUBA?t=218)).

## 10. What this means for PitCrew

1. **Run diagnosis in priority order.** Check settings (§9) first, then braking consistency, then minimum speed and rotation, then line and track use, then trail-braking refinement, then racecraft. Only coach a rung once the rung below it passes.
2. **Give one focus per session.** Choose the fault with the largest estimated time loss that the driver's current rung can act on. Hide lap time during single-skill drills.
3. **Make the headline score about control.** Lead with consistency (brake-point σ, lap σ) and minimum-speed delta. Lap time sits underneath.
4. **Explain why, not just what.** Every cue links to a principle and a timestamped video moment from §5. That turns imitation into understanding.
5. **Use setup as a coaching lever.** Track the driver's spin rate and correction rate. Suggest a calmer setup when confidence drops, and a more aggressive one to force precision when the driver plateaus.
6. **Plan for practice in races.** Encourage repeated races on a fixed car-track combination, short 30–45 minute blocks, and spaced review of past focus items.
7. **Build in the mindset layer.** Use task-framed language in all copy, nudge the driver toward a 90% stint after spins, and turn post-incident replays into ownership prompts.
